/free and open project management for everyone/

LucidPlan is a project management system for teams of all sizes and all kinds
of work: a multi-project workspace, a priority-ordered backlog, and drag-and-drop
kanban boards, kept deliberately simple and hackable end to end in Scheme.

It's a flexible system made to simplify everyone's life, accessible to all kinds
of users -- and free software (free as in freedom), licensed under the
GNU Affero General Public License v3 or newer.

* What LucidPlan is
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-lucidplan-is
:END:

LucidPlan enhances the use of Agile work methodologies in teams thanks to its
fast-paced, no-nonsense workflows and deep customizability, also thanks to being
written in Lisp.

This tool is the fruit of years of experience using bloated proprietary systems
like Jira and Trello, and experiencing the insane frustrations they bring.

** Who it is for
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: who-it-is-for
:END:

- *Small teams* that want a board and a backlog today, not a six-week rollout.
- *Dozens of projects at once*: one workspace, one dashboard, one search box.
- *Lisp people*, who can make the thing behave differently by writing a few
  lines of Scheme instead of filing a feature request.
- *Self-hosters and freedom-lovers*, who want their team's work on their own
  machine, under their own licence, without an account anywhere else.

** What it is built with
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-it-is-built-with
:END:

Built with [[https://www.gnu.org/software/guile/][Guile Scheme]] and [[https://artanis.dev/][GNU Artanis]], [[https://sqlite.org/][SQLite]], [[https://codeberg.org/jjba23/olive-css][Olive CSS]] and some small sprinkles of vanilla JavaScript.

* Features
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: features
:END:

LucidPlan provides a comprehensive suite of tools to keep your projects
organized:

- *Multi-Project Workspaces*: Easily manage dozens of repositories or projects
  from a single unified dashboard. Features a quick-access "go to" dropdown and
  search functionality to navigate between contexts instantly.
- *Versatile Backlog Management*: View all your project tasks in a clean,
  list-based backlog with priority drag and drop. Tickets have different types
  (=Task=, =Bug=, =Story=, =Spike=, =Time Box=), configurable statuses, org-mode
  syntax descriptions with links and rich content, and easy editing/deletion.
- *Kanban Boards*: Visualize your workflow with built-in Kanban boards.
  Effortlessly track tickets and drag and drop as they move through columns like
  =To-do=, =Work in Progress=, and =In Review=.
- *Clean and Distraction-Free UI*: A minimalist and accessible interface built
  with [[https://codeberg.org/jjba23/olive-css][Olive CSS]], featuring global search, quick project creation, and a
  toggleable light/dark mode to suit your preferences.
- *Granular User Management*: Add, edit, and manage team members with ease.
  Permissions are handled using native Lisp lists (e.g.,
  ~`(lucidplan:ticket-create lucidplan:project-delete)~), allowing for
  incredibly fine-grained, programmatic access control, effectively providing an
  easy to use [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Access-control_list][ACL]] with which you can control exactly what each user can/cannot
  do.
- *Org mode support*: Titles and descriptions can be written using Org mode
  syntax, which enables more advanced media. This Org text gets automatically
  rendered to attractive [[https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/][HTML]].
- *Flexible statuses*: the statuses a ticket moves through are configuration,
  not code -- rename them, drop the ones your team does not use, add your own,
  or give a single project its own board.
- *Email notifications*: a new ticket is announced to everyone taking part in
  the project.
- *A [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representational_state_transfer][REST]] [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/API][API]]*: =/api/v1= hands tickets and projects to your scripts, with the
  same permissions the web interface uses.
- *Fully Customizable*: Everything an instance says about itself -- its name,
  its description, its footer, the address mail is sent from -- is
  configuration, and the behaviour and content of everything else can be hacked
  with simple Lisp (Scheme) code.
- *Cross-platform*: Runs anywhere that Guile Scheme can run, and exposes web
  endpoints, and is thus accessible from computers, phones, etc.
- *Free Software*: Contribute to its development or extend it with your own
  custom features.

* Using LucidPlan
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: using-lucidplan
:END:

** Backlog Management
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: backlog-management
:END:

LucidPlan provides several ways to organize and work with tickets directly from
the web interface. Ticket management is designed around fast, simple
interactions.

Tickets in the backlog can be reordered (in terms of priority) using native
browser drag-and-drop. A rearrangement is written as the tickets' priorities, so
the grip is offered while the backlog is sorted by priority -- ~Priority -
Descending~ or ~Priority - Ascending~ -- and each direction writes the numbers
that keep the rows where they were put. Under any other sort the row would slide
back to its old place on the next visit, so no grip is offered there; pick a
priority sort to reorder.

Backlog tickets can be expanded and collapsed without navigating away from the
page, singly or all at once with *Expand All* and *Collapse All*.

Each ticket contains an expandable details section containing information such
as:

- description;
- status;
- type;
- priority;
- assignee;
- project/ticket identifier;
- edit and delete actions.

The backlog also carries a one-glance summary of the project -- how many tickets
are open, done and rejected, how many there are in total, and a completion
bar -- plus a sort menu and a search box.

** Kanban Management
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: kanban-management
:END:

The Kanban board also provides a drag-and-drop interaction. Dragging a ticket
onto another Kanban column changes its status.

For example, a ticket can be moved from:

#+name: status-flow
#+begin_example
To-do → Work in Progress → In Review → Done
#+end_example

** Keyboard Accessibility
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: keyboard-accessibility
:END:

Ticket and project lists are fully keyboard accessible using =Tab= navigation
with ~Enter~ or ~Space~ for toggling visibility. The UI aims to be accessible to
all kinds of users and usage patterns.

** Practical usage tips
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: practical-usage-tips
:END:

Using LucidPlan is made to be easy and simple; here are some tips that might
help you out.

- In any ~title~ or ~description~ field you can use the Org mode syntax (like in
  the [[https://www.gnu.org/software/emacs/][Emacs]] Org mode) and LucidPlan will automatically convert your markup to
  pretty HTML.
- The theme button in the header switches between light and dark, and remembers
  your choice.
- The *go to* menu in the header jumps straight to any project's board.
- Drag a ticket by its grip (the little dotted handle), never by the card
  itself, so that selecting the text of a ticket still means selecting it.

** How the pages treat a reader who is not signed in
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: how-the-pages-treat-a-reader-who-is-not-signed-in
:END:

LucidPlan is readable before it is writable. A signed-out reader can browse the
front page, the about page, every project's backlog and board, and each
ticket -- but every page that exists to submit or change something is behind the
permission that change actually needs, and turns the reader away *before* they
fill in a form that could only ever refuse them.

The pages that create or edit something ask for the same permission their write
does:

- creating or editing a project -- ~lucidplan:project-create~;
- creating or editing a ticket -- ~lucidplan:ticket-create~;
- managing users -- ~lucidplan:modify-permissions~.

The small inline controls on a ticket (its status and type) are shown to exactly
the readers the endpoint behind them would accept, so a reader who holds the
permission sees the control and a reader who does not sees the read-only badge.

** What the mail service is asked to send
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-the-mail-service-is-asked-to-send
:END:

An instance that has been given a [[https://codeberg.org/jjba23/pingwing][pingwing]] address (~pingwing-url~ and its two
credentials, see [[#configuring-the-instance][Configuring the instance]]) writes to the people of a project when a
ticket is created in it -- the news that a board moved is worth having even when
nobody is looking at it.

Who hears it is everyone who takes part in the project: every user the project
has assigned a ticket to, together with the ticket's own assignee and the person
who opened it. LucidPlan keeps no membership list, so participation is read from
the work itself; a user with no email address is passed over, and an address is
written to once however many times it comes up.

The message is pingwing's ~lucidplan-new-ticket~ template, filled with the
project, the ticket's number and title, its type, its status as the project's own
board names it, its priority, the assignee and the author, the description, and a
link to the ticket on ~site-url~. The subject is the instance's own
~mail-subject-prefix~ and then ~New ticket in <project>~; the sender is
~mail-sender-name~ and ~mail-sender-address~.

Nothing about this can fail a write: a mail service that is unreachable, or that
refuses the task, is tried again a few times (~mail-retry-attempts~,
~mail-retry-delay~) and then given up on with a line in the log that repeats what
the service said, and the ticket is already saved either way. A message counts as
sent only when the service answers that it took it: a request that arrived and
was refused is a message that did not go, and is treated as one.

* Running LucidPlan yourself
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: running-lucidplan-yourself

If you are thinking of running LucidPlan yourself and hosting it on your own,

** Quick start (the impatient path)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: quick-start-the-impatient-path
:END:

If you already have [[https://guix.gnu.org/][GNU Guix]], this is the whole thing:

#+begin_src sh
git clone https://codeberg.org/jjba23/lucidplan.git
cd lucidplan
guix shell -m manifest.scm            # every dependency, pinned
maak init-db                          # create the database and migrate it
maak dev                              # hot-reloading server on localhost:50025
#+end_src

Then seed yourself a first account ([[#creating-the-first-super-user][Creating the first super-user]] has the details)
and sign in at [[http://localhost:50025/login]]. ~maak --list~ shows every other task.

** Configuring the instance
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: configuring-the-instance
:END:

An instance keeps its private configuration in a module called ~(lucidplan env)~,
which LucidPlan loads at start-up from a file named ~env.scm~ in the root of the
project. That file is *per-deployment and must not be committed* (it is already
in ~.gitignore~). Create it:

#+begin_src scheme
(define-module (lucidplan env))

;; -- Secrets: unique to this deployment ------------------------------

;; Encrypts the session token. Generate a fresh UUID for each key.
(define-public encryption-key "a2ebe4c5-4a78-4490-af83-cfb05a285bf9")
(define-public signing-key "8d3a5bcc-973e-4f80-aa3d-33be33d6631c")

;; -- Mail: how password resets and invites are delivered ---------------
;; Pingwing is a mail and notification service written in Guile Scheme,
;; also part of the jointhefreeworld project. Find it at
;; https://codeberg.org/jjba23/pingwing -- without it, invites and
;; password resets cannot be delivered.
(define-public pingwing-url "https://pingwing.example.org")
(define-public pingwing-basic-auth-user "my-user")
(define-public pingwing-basic-auth-password "my-password")

;; -- Site: what this instance says about itself ------------------------
;; Every value below is optional; omit any line to keep LucidPlan's
;; default. See the table further down for the whole list.
(define-public site-name "LucidPlan")
(define-public site-url "https://lucidplan.example.org")
(define-public contact-email "admin@example.org")
(define-public copyright-holder "Your Name or Organisation")
#+end_src

Every key above is read by ~(lucidplan config)~ at start-up: a value defined in
the env module wins, and anything you leave out falls back to the default that
ships with LucidPlan. See [[#instance-configuration-keys][Instance configuration keys]] for the full list.

If you run LucidPlan locally, it is recommended to use ~localhost~ rather than
~127.0.0.1~, as this works better with the authentication, authorization and
other cookies.

#+begin_src scheme

** Renaming or changing the ticket statuses
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: renaming-or-changing-the-ticket-statuses
:END:

Which statuses a ticket moves through is configuration, not code: an instance
that does not use every status drops the ones it does not need, gives the ones
it keeps the names its team says, and adds any that are missing.

The key is ~ticket-statuses~, a list of entries. Each entry names one status and
means ~(value label icon glyph bucket)~:

- ~value~ -- what the database stores, e.g. ~"todo"~. Keep it stable: renaming a
  status means changing its ~label~;
- ~label~ -- what a reader is shown, e.g. ~"To-do"~;
- ~icon~ -- the name of a [[https://phosphoricons.com/][Phosphor icon]], e.g. ~"anchor"~;
- ~glyph~ -- one character, used in the menus a page cannot dress;
- ~bucket~ -- ~"open"~, ~"done"~ or ~"rejected"~. Open statuses are the board's
  columns and the backlog's live list; the closed ones become their own sections
  and their own counts.

So an instance whose team works in a speak-now-or-forever flow might say:

#+begin_src scheme
(define-public ticket-statuses
  '(("backlog" "Backlog" "tray" "📥" "open")
    ("doing" "Doing" "gear-fine" "⚙️" "open")
    ("review" "In Review" "list-magnifying-glass" "🔍" "open")
    ("blocked" "Blocked" "prohibit" "🚫" "open")
    ("shipped" "Shipped" "check" "✅" "done")
    ("wontfix" "Won't Fix" "x" "❌" "rejected")))
#+end_src

Two things follow from the list being the instance's:

- the *order* of the entries is the order of the board's columns and of every
  status menu;
- a status that is dropped from the list does not make its tickets disappear. A
  ticket carrying a value the list does not name is treated as open, so it stays
  on the board and can be dragged back onto a status that exists.

A status whose label is one of the shipped ones ([[#instance-configuration-keys][the list above]] keeps
~"To-do"~, ~"Work in Progress"~ and so on) is a translation key and is shown in
the reader's own language; a label of your own is shown exactly as written.

** A project with its own statuses
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: a-project-with-its-own-statuses
:END:

A single instance can host very different projects, and one team's board is not
always another's. So the instance's ~ticket-statuses~ is a *default*: any project
may override the whole list for itself, from its own edit page, in the same
one-status-to-a-line format:

#+name: status-labels
#+begin_example
todo | Backlog | anchor | ⚓ | open
work-in-progress | Doing | gear-fine | ⚙️ | open
done | Shipped | check | ✅ | done
#+end_example

The fields are ~value | label | icon | glyph | bucket~, in that order, and
everything but the value may be left out -- the value is then also the label, the
icon is a dot and the bucket is ~open~. A line beginning with ~#~ is a comment, so
the editor is also a place to leave a note. Leaving the field empty gives the
project back the instance's list.

Nothing else has to change: the project's backlog, its board, its ticket pages
and its status menus are all built from whichever list the project ends up with.

** The database
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-database
:END:

LucidPlan needs a database in the root folder of the project (alongside ~app~,
~lib~, etc.). It uses SQLite by default, and is also compatible with [[https://www.postgresql.org/][PostgreSQL]]
and [[https://www.mysql.com/][MySQL]].

If you use SQLite you should enable [[https://sqlite.org/wal.html][WAL mode]] for concurrent writes. The ~maak~
task does that for you (and creates the file):

#+begin_src sh
maak init-db
#+end_src

Schema migrations are managed with [[https://codeberg.org/jjba23/mutastructura][mutastructura]], and they are applied with one task:

#+begin_src sh
maak migrate
#+end_src

~maak init-db~ makes a fresh database and migrates it in one go, so a new
instance never has to think about this. On a database that already exists,
~maak migrate~ applies whatever it has not seen and leaves the rest alone --
running it twice in a row is a no-op, and running it on a current database prints
~NOTHING TO DO!~.

A migration is a plain [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQL][SQL]] file under ~resources/migrations/sqlite/~, named
~YYYY-MM-DD_HH:MM:SS_something.sql~: mutastructura applies the files it has not
seen in filename order, each in its own transaction, so a migration that fails
leaves the schema where it was rather than half-moved. What it has seen is kept
in a ~migrations_history~ table -- the ledger -- with the [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHA-2][SHA-256]] of each file,
so editing a migration that has already run is reported as a
~WARNING: Checksum mismatch~ instead of being applied a second time. The ledger
is the reason migrations are applied by ~maak migrate~ and not by hand: a
database migrated by hand has no ledger, and its schema can no longer be compared
with the migrations.

An existing deployment that was set up before LucidPlan used mutastructura has
one of those unledgered databases. Bring it up to date once, by hand, by
recording what is already applied -- the migration files are not re-run, and
nothing in the database changes:

#+begin_src sh
# Run this once, on an instance that was migrated by hand:
sqlite3 lucidplan.db "CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS migrations_history (
    id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT,
    name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    checksum VARCHAR(64) NOT NULL,
    applied_at DATETIME DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
    UNIQUE (name, checksum))"
for f in resources/migrations/sqlite/*.sql; do
  sqlite3 lucidplan.db "INSERT OR IGNORE INTO migrations_history (name, checksum) VALUES (
      '$(basename "$f")',
      '$(sha256sum "$f" | cut -d' ' -f1)'))"
done
#+end_src

After that, ~maak migrate~ answers ~NOTHING TO DO!~ and every later migration is
applied by the one task.

** Creating the first super-user
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: creating-the-first-super-user
:END:

A brand-new LucidPlan has no users at all, and the user-management pages need a
user to reach them -- so the very first account is seeded directly into the
database, by hand.

A super-user is simply a user whose permission list contains
~lucidplan:modify-permissions~: that permission lets its holder grant every other
permission to themselves and to others, so it is the root of the whole ACL.

1. Pick a password and a salt (any string works; the salt just has to be kept
   with the account).
2. Compute the stored password, which is the *SHA-256 of =password + salt=*
   (password first, salt appended, no separator). You can compute it from the
   same shell you run the project in:

#+begin_src sh
# With the exact Guile the project uses (inside `guix shell -m manifest.scm`):
guile -c '(use-modules (artanis security nss)) (display (string->sha-256 (string-append "my-password" "my-personal-salt"))) (newline)'
#+end_src

3. Insert the row:

#+begin_src sql
INSERT INTO "user" (
    id,           -- any unique UUID for this account
    username,
    email,        -- the address you will sign in with
    password,     -- the SHA-256 computed above
    salt,         -- the salt you used above
    permissions   -- a back-quoted Lisp list of symbols
) VALUES (
    '2f6a4d3e-1c2b-4a5f-9e8d-0b7c6a5d4e3f',
    'Joe',
    'you@example.org',
    'the-sha256-you-computed',
    'my-personal-salt',
    '`(lucidplan:modify-permissions lucidplan:ticket-create lucidplan:ticket-delete lucidplan:project-create lucidplan:project-delete lucidplan:user-create lucidplan:user-delete)'
);
#+end_src

The ~permissions~ column is read back with Lisp's ~eval-string~, so it must be
the *text of a back-quoted list* -- that is, it starts with a backtick (`` ` ``)
and contains a Scheme list of the ~lucidplan:...~ symbols. The list above is the
full set; give the super-user all of them.

#+begin_src sh
sqlite3 lucidplan.db "INSERT INTO \"user\" (id, username, email, password, salt, permissions) VALUES ('2f6a4d3e-1c2b-4a5f-9e8d-0b7c6a5d4e3f','Joe','you@example.org','THE_SHA256','my-personal-salt','\`(lucidplan:modify-permissions lucidplan:ticket-create lucidplan:ticket-delete lucidplan:project-create lucidplan:project-delete lucidplan:user-create lucidplan:user-delete)');"
#+end_src

** Running it
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: running-it
:END:

If you are hacking away at the project, use the development target: it runs the
server with hot-reloading, using the default ~artanis.conf~ where hostname,
database path, etc. are configured.

#+begin_src sh
maak dev      # hot-reloading development server (alias: maak hr)
maak server   # plain server with the default configuration
#+end_src

By default the server listens on ~localhost:50025~.

For production, use the production configuration (which sets the real host name)
and, ideally, run LucidPlan as a [[https://www.gnu.org/software/shepherd/][Shepherd]] service so it starts on boot and is
restarted if it falls over.

#+begin_src sh
maak production-server
#+end_src

Here follows an example Shepherd service for a Guix System:

#+begin_src scheme
(define-module (wolk-jjba lucidplan)
  #:use-module (gnu)
  #:use-module (guix))

(use-service-modules shepherd)

(define-public (wolk-jjba-lucidplan-service config)
  (list (shepherd-service (documentation "Run lucidplan as a daemon")
                          (provision '(lucidplan))
                          (requirement '())
                          (start #~(make-forkexec-constructor '("maak"
                                                                "production-server")
                                    #:directory
                                    "/etc/lucidplan/job-clone/lucidplan/trunk"
                                    #:environment-variables '("GUILE_LOAD_PATH=/run/current-system/profile/share/guile/site/3.0"
                                                              "GUILE_DBD_PATH=/run/current-system/profile/lib"
                                                              "C_INCLUDE_PATH=/run/current-system/profile/include"
                                                              "GUILE_LOAD_COMPILED_PATH=/run/current-system/profile/lib/guile/3.0/site-ccache:/run/current-system/profile/share/guile/site/3.0"
                                                              "LIBRARY_PATH=/run/current-system/profile/lib"
                                                              "GIT_SSL_NO_VERIFY=1"
                                                              "PATH=/run/privileged/bin:/run/current-system/profile/bin:/run/current-system/profile/sbin")))
                          (stop #~(make-kill-destructor))
                          (auto-start? #t)
                          (respawn? #t))))

(define-public wolk-jjba-lucidplan-service-type
  (service-type (name 'lucidplan)
                (description "Run lucidplan as a daemon")
                (extensions (list (service-extension
                                   shepherd-root-service-type
                                   wolk-jjba-lucidplan-service)))
                (default-value '())))


#+end_src

** Creating the other users
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: creating-the-other-users
:END:

Once you can sign in as the super-user, the rest of the team is created from the
interface, not from SQL:

1. Sign in at ~/login~ with the super-user you seeded.
2. Open *user management* (the ~/users/manage~ page, linked from your profile page
   at ~/me~).
3. Press *new user*, give the account a username and an email, tick the
   permissions it should hold, and create it.

A new account is created with *no password*. Instead, LucidPlan sends its owner an
email containing a one-time link (a password reset) with which they set their own
password. An account is therefore created by the operator and *claimed* by its
owner -- the operator never chooses somebody else's password.

For that email to arrive, the mail service (~pingwing-url~ and its credentials,
above) has to be configured and reachable. If a message could not be delivered,
the account still exists and the invite can be sent again: on the user-management
page every account has a *resend invite* button, and the page tells you plainly
when a message failed to go out. Messages are also retried a few times before
giving up.

To give a single person the ability to manage users, tick
~lucidplan:modify-permissions~ for them. To let someone create and edit tickets
but nothing else, tick only ~lucidplan:ticket-create~.

** Host name, TLS and search engines
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: host-name-tls-and-search-engines
:END:

For a public instance, point ~host.name~ (in ~conf/production.conf~) at your
domain and put LucidPlan behind a reverse proxy that terminates [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Layer_Security][TLS]], since the
session cookie is set ~secure~ and will not be stored over plain HTTP. Set
~site-url~ in ~env.scm~ to the public address so the canonical links and preview
images are absolute.

Search-engine basics are handled for you: every page carries a title, a
description, a canonical address and Open Graph/Twitter preview tags, plus a
[[https://json-ld.org/][JSON-LD]] block describing the application. ~/robots.txt~ is generated and points
at a ~/sitemap.xml~ that lists the public pages (the front page, the about page,
the project list, and each project's backlog and board).

** The theme, and the files it is made of
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-theme-and-the-files-it-is-made-of
:END:

LucidPlan wears a theme it serves itself. The Lucida faces, the two web font
families the stylesheet names after them, the Phosphor icon face, the stylesheets
that describe them, and the image a link preview shows are all files of this
project, under ~pub/css/~, ~pub/fonts/~ and ~pub/img/~. A page load therefore asks
this instance for them and no other host: nothing about your visitors is sent to
a [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_delivery_network][CDN]] or a font service, and nothing about your instance is told to anybody you
did not choose.

The files are committed, so a fresh checkout serves them without any extra step.
They are refreshed from where they came from by one task, which names each source:

#+begin_src sh
maak fetch-assets
#+end_src

Where the instance hands them out is configuration. Left alone, a page names them
by path -- ~/css/fonts.css~ -- and the instance that served the page answers for
them, which is what a self-hosted deployment wants. Set ~asset-url~ in ~env.scm~
to an absolute base to hand that work to a static host or a CDN instead:

#+begin_src scheme
(define-public asset-url "https://assets.example.org/lucidplan")
#+end_src

Page analytics is also yours to decide. The instance's own page-view script
reports to ~analytics-endpoint~, and setting it empty means the script is not put
on the page at all -- no request leaves the reader's browser:

#+begin_src scheme
(define-public analytics-endpoint "")
#+end_src

** Translations
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: translations
:END:

The interface is translated with [[https://www.gnu.org/software/gettext/][gettext]]. The catalogs live under
~sys/i18n/locale/~. The languages offered today are English (US), Nederlands,
Español and 日本語. When you add translatable strings (they are written as
~(G_ "...")~), refresh and compile the catalogs:

#+begin_src sh
maak i18n-update-template   # collect new strings into the .pot template
maak i18n-merge             # merge the template into every .po
maak i18n-compile           # compile the .po files into the .mo the server reads
#+end_src

The languages offered in the interface are configuration: set ~locales~ in
~env.scm~ to the catalogs you actually have.

* Authentication, Authorization, User management
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: authentication-authorization-user-management
:END:

[[https://codeberg.org/jjba23/bewaking][bewaking]] 🔗 is a nice library to help you secure your web applications with
Guile Scheme.

With bewaking we use secure tokens encrypted and stored in secure cookies (for
browsers) and headers (for REST API) as well as authentication and authorization,
with ACLs (access control list) and fine-grained permissions.

If you want to be a super user/root user in lucidplan your token's permissions
list will need to contain the ~lucidplan:modify-permissions~ permission,
effectively meaning you can give yourself and others all wanted permissions and
do everything.

LucidPlan's permission model ACL:
- ~lucidplan:ticket-create~
- ~lucidplan:ticket-delete~
- ~lucidplan:project-create~
- ~lucidplan:project-delete~
- ~lucidplan:user-create~
- ~lucidplan:user-delete~
- ~lucidplan:modify-permissions~

LucidPlan requires an authenticated session for performing administrative tasks
such as creating/deleting tickets and projects. For web browsers you can simply
use the login page; for scripts and headless usage, use the ~/api/v1/login~
endpoint and take the ~x-bewaking-lucidplan~ response header.

** The REST API
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-rest-api
:END:

The versioned API lives under ~/api/v1~ and speaks [[https://www.json.org/][JSON]]. All of it is guarded by
the same ACL as the pages, so a token carries exactly the permissions its owner
has.

|--------------------------+------------------------------+-------------------------------------------|
| *Method and path*        | *Answer*                     | *Notes*                                   |
|--------------------------+------------------------------+-------------------------------------------|
| ~POST /api/v1/login~     | ~204~ and the token, or ~401~ | Body: ~email~ and ~password~, URL-encoded |
| ~GET /api/v1/projects~   | the project list             | Token header needed                       |
| ~GET /api/v1/tickets/:project~ | a project's tickets    | ~:project~ is the project's human id      |
|--------------------------+------------------------------+-------------------------------------------|

#+begin_src sh
# Sign in and keep the token the service answers with:
TOKEN=$(curl -s -D - -o /dev/null -X POST \
  -d 'email=you@example.org&password=your-password' \
  http://localhost:50025/api/v1/login | awk '/[Xx]-bewaking-lucidplan/{print $2}' | tr -d '\r')

# Ask for a project's tickets with it:
curl -s -H "x-bewaking-lucidplan: $TOKEN" \
  http://localhost:50025/api/v1/tickets/lucidplan
#+end_src

** On Reproducibility: Why GNU Guix?
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: on-reproducibility-why-gnu-guix
:END:

A build should never fail because "it worked on my machine." Reliable software
development requires explicit, bit-for-bit reproducibility and a functional
approach to package management and system deployment.

#+BEGIN_EXPORT html
<a href="https://guix.gnu.org/">
  <img src="https://jointhefreeworld.org/ggg/dist/gnu-guix.svg" alt="GNU Guix logo" style="max-width: 250px; width: 100%; height: auto;" />
</a>
#+END_EXPORT

- *Declarative Build Graphs:* Packages are pure functions of their inputs with no implicit system state.
- *Programmable with Guile:* Full system and package configurations written in [[https://www.gnu.org/software/guile/][Guile Scheme]] rather than rigid DSLs or static files.
- *Time Travel:* Rebuild the exact system state from any point in [[https://git-scm.com/][git]] history using ~guix time-machine~.
- *Atomic Operations:* Instantly upgrade or roll back environments without leaving orphaned dependencies or broken states.
- *Unified Tooling:* One tool manages development shells, isolated containers, package builds, and full system configurations.

** On GNU Artanis
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: on-gnu-artanis
:END:

Find the documentation for Artanis here: https://www.gnu.org/software/artanis/manual/

GNU Artanis is the first production-level modern Web framework of the Scheme programming language.

It is designed and maintained to be robust, fast, and easy to use for professional web development.

Here lies not just a framework, but a canvas where your ideas take flight with remarkable speed and grace. Artanis isn't about wrestling with complexity; it's about the joy of creation.

It's about harnessing the power of a robust and mature language to build something truly your own, something beautiful, nimble, flexible, powerful, efficient, understandable and maintainable. GNU Artanis was Certificated as Awesome Project at 2013 Lisp in summer projects.

One day, the folks at GNU were discussing what language they would write the GNU website in - and many chose [[https://www.python.org/][Python]]. But I found that strange, because the official extension language of GNU is GNU Guile. And I wondered aloud - why not start a brand new project to provide a web framework written with GNU Guile? To which [[https://stallman.org/][RMS]] said, "It's cool, I like this idea."

But at that time, it was just an idea without a plan.

Fortunately, a few months later, the Guile community held a hack-potluck to celebrate Guile2 turning two - which is a contest to write a cool program in a few weeks. And so, Artanis was born.

History:

    February 2013 - Artanis born at the GNU Guile hack-potluck.
    2013 - Artanis submitted to "Lisp In Summer Projects" contest. Received "Certificated awesome project" award in 2014.
    1st January, 2015 - the first stable version Artanis-0.0.1 was released.
    19th January, 2015 - offers Artanis to [[https://www.fsf.org/][FSF]]/GNU, and RMS inducts it as an official GNU project. Artanis becomes GNU Artanis.
    29 December 2021 - GNU Artanis donates to HardenedLinux community to connect with product environment more tightly.
